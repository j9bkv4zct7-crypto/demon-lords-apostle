Chapter 3

A Kitchen Full of Rumors

1,572 words · about 7 min

‹ Chapter 2 Contents Interlude: The Empty Seat ›

Hunger hit me halfway back to my room, all at once, the way it does after you've spent a day turning your body into a weapon and forgotten to feed it.

I went looking for help. The castle at night was all blue torchlight and long shadows. Ogres lumbered past, goblins scurried by, and skeletal sentries stood motionless at their posts. None of them looked like they took dinner orders.

Then I spotted her.

She was short and round, barely five feet tall, with lavender skin, long floppy ears, and enormous dark eyes. Silver curls escaped from under a white cloth cap, and she wore a brown dress with an apron that had clearly survived a great many sauces. She was waddling down the corridor with a tray of empty bowls, humming to herself.

"Excuse me," I said.

She jumped and nearly lost the tray. The bowls rattled as she spun around and blinked up at me.

"Oh! Oh my!" She clutched the tray to her chest. "You're the human! The Apostle! Everyone's been talking about you all day!" She bobbed something that might have been a curtsy, and the bowls wobbled dangerously. "Bris Hollowpot! I work the kitchens! Cook, clean, scrub, serve. You name it, Bris does it!"

Her nose twitched as she looked me over.

"You look half dead and fully starved, if you don't mind my saying. Come, come. The kitchen's just down this way. We've got roasted boar, black bread, mushroom stew, good hearty stuff that'll put some meat on those human bones of yours."

She set off without waiting.

"Lead the way, Bris," I said, falling in behind her. "My hunger is as vast as my ambition."

Her laugh bubbled up, and her ears bounced as she picked up speed. "Oh, I like you already! Most of the demons around here just grunt and point. It's nice to have someone with manners, even if you are a human."

The kitchen lay down a winding staircase in the warm belly of the castle. I smelled it before I saw it: roasted meat, herbs, woodsmoke, and something sweet in an oven. It was enormous, a vaulted stone ceiling over rows of iron stoves and open fire pits, with great pots bubbling and a small army of imps, lavender and grey, darting between them with ladles and cleavers.

"Sit, sit, sit!" Bris steered me to a thick wooden table in the corner and vanished into the steam.

She came back with a platter heaped high: a thick slab of roasted boar glistening with fat and charred at the edges, a bowl of dark mushroom stew with chunks of root vegetable, a hunk of dense black bread, and a tall clay mug of something amber and warm.

"Demon ale," she said, tapping the mug. "Weak batch. Won't knock a human out." She considered. "Probably."

She hopped onto the stool across from me, propped her chin in her hands, and watched me with frank, vibrating curiosity.

"So. Is it true you blew a minotaur's head clean off this morning? The goblins won't stop chattering about it."

"I did." I tore into the boar. It was very good, rich and tender and seasoned with herbs I couldn't name. "I'd like a sample of every meat you have. And fruit. I want a strong body, but a lean one."

Her eyes widened further, which I wouldn't have believed was possible.

"Every meat! Every fruit! You've an appetite fit for an ogre!" She clapped her small lavender hands. "Bris can do that. Bris can absolutely do that!"

She scurried off, barking orders in a high, rapid dialect I somehow understood perfectly. Apparently that came with the Apostle package.

"Tansy! Slice the smoked wyrm jerky! Moggle, the salted basilisk flank from the cold cellar! And somebody pull those ember berries and frost plums before they turn!"

The second platter arrived with a proud little huff. There were thin slices of dark purple wyrm jerky, pale and dense basilisk flank, strips of grilled shadow deer so dark they were nearly black, and small cuts of salted wyvern breast. Beside them sat a bowl of fruit: ember berries that glowed orange and felt warm in my hand, frost plums with blue, icy skins, and little red starfruit that smelled faintly of honey.

"Wyrm and basilisk are pure lean muscle," Bris explained, pointing at each. "Shadow deer too. Wyvern's got more fat, but it's good fat. The fruits are packed with mana nutrients. Ember berries help your stamina come back. Frost plums help your mana." She beamed. "Eat up, Apostle. Bris's kitchen makes warriors."

I ate like a man who'd just discovered he could convert calories into levels, which I suspected I could.

"I appreciate your hospitality, Bris," I said eventually. "Tell me. What do you know about the higher ranks of this castle?"

Her ears twitched. She glanced around the kitchen, saw that the other imps were absorbed in their work, and leaned across the table, dropping her squeak to something like a whisper.

"Well. I know a fair bit. Forty years in these kitchens. You hear things when nobody thinks you're listening."

She held up one stubby finger.

"First, there's His Majesty. Lord Zarvoth. He's ruled more than a thousand years. Nobody knows how old he really is, and nobody asks twice."

A second finger.

"Then Commander Shiryeon. His right hand. She runs the army. Two hundred years of service, fiercely loyal. Some say she'd die for him without blinking. The soldiers fear her more than most of the generals."

A third finger.

"Above the regular forces are the Four Pillars. His Majesty's four strongest, beneath only himself. Each one commands a part of the army." Her voice dropped even lower. "I only know two by name. General Valgrimm, a great dragon-born, leads the front line against the Celestine Empire. And Lady Ysveth, an elder dark-elf sorceress. Intelligence. Spies." She shook her head. "The other two keep to themselves. Hardly ever seen in the castle."

She picked up a frost plum and nibbled at it nervously.

"Why do you ask, Apostle? Planning something already?"

"Only curious." I leaned back. "Where can I find a female demon to attend me in my chambers after dinner?"

Bris froze with the plum at her lips. She blinked once, then twice. Then her lavender cheeks flushed a deep, alarming purple.

"I... well... that's..."

The kitchen door banged open. A goblin runner tumbled through it, tripping over its own feet and clutching a scroll.

"Apostle Mephesto!" it shrieked, gasping. "Message from Commander Shiryeon! Urgent!"

It shoved the scroll into my hands and collapsed against a table, wheezing. Across from me, Bris let out a breath of such enormous relief that her ears drooped, then began gathering empty bowls with supernatural speed.

The handwriting was sharp and precise.

Apostle. Report to the war room at first light. His Majesty has accelerated your training schedule. We move to the inner ring tomorrow. Eat well and sleep. You'll need both.

— Shiryeon

P.S. Stop harassing the castle staff.

I looked up. Somehow, impossibly, she already knew.

Bris was now on the far side of the kitchen scrubbing a pot with tremendous concentration, her ears flat against her head, refusing to look anywhere near me.

"More food, Apostle?" she called, without turning around. "I can wrap some wyrm jerky for your room! Very good for you! Take it and go and rest! Big day tomorrow! Very big! Goodnight!"

A bundle of jerky and frost plums landed in my arms, and a surprisingly strong pair of small hands pushed me toward the door.

"Very well, Bris." I let her herd me out, smirking. "Sleep well. Tomorrow I begin my ascent to power."

She waved frantically from behind the counter, her ears bouncing. "Yes, yes! Ascend! Conquer! Just do it after a full night's rest! Goodnight, Apostle!"

The door shut behind me.

I walked back through the quiet corridors chewing a strip of wyrm jerky. It was tough and smoky, and the flavor lingered. The goblins had retreated wherever goblins go at night, and the skeletal knights tracked me with their hollow eyes as I passed.

My room was as I'd left it. Through the narrow window the red sky was fading to black, and strange stars were coming out in constellations no one on Earth had ever named.

I sat on the edge of the bed. My muscles were sore and my mana was still climbing back. But the cold core in my chest pulsed steadily, noticeably stronger than it had been that morning.

Level seven in one day. Tomorrow was the inner ring. The monsters would be stronger, and the experience richer.

I lay back on the dark sheets and thought of them: twenty faces from Lecture Hall 3, risen into gold while I fell into red. Somewhere far across this continent, in a white palace full of warm light, they'd be standing in a row receiving blessings from a goddess. They'd be wondering, maybe, where the twenty-first had gone.

They had no idea where I was, or what I was becoming.

A laugh came up out of my chest, low and not entirely pleasant. It echoed off the stone.

"Soon," I told the dark, "the light of their world will be extinguished by my shadow."

The cold core pulsed once, as if in agreement.

Then sleep took me, deep and dreamless and heavy as stone.
