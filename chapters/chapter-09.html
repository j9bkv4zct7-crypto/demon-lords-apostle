Chapter 9

A Head on the War Table

3,009 words · about 13 min

‹ Interlude: The Report Contents Chapter 10 ›

"I'm reporting to the Demon Lord," I told Skeev. "Follow behind."

He fell into step at once, his short legs working double-time, his eyes darting back and forth between the head and my face in a mixture of horror and admiration.

"Master finished the mission already? Master left this morning! Skeev thought it would take days!" He scrambled around a corner after me. "The other generals take weeks to do siege work. Weeks! Master does it in one night!"

Servants and lesser demons flattened themselves against the walls as we passed. A figure carrying a human head through the obsidian halls tends to clear a path. Word was already traveling ahead of us. By the time we reached the war room, I could hear the whispers rippling through the castle behind us.

One of the two armored guards at the doors knocked twice.

"Enter."

The war room was lit by floating crimson orbs. The Demon Lord stood at the far end with his back to us, studying a large map of the western front pinned to the wall. The red light caught his golden hair and slid along the curve of his horns.

He didn't turn right away.

"You were given three days." His voice was quiet and measured. "It has been one."

Then he turned, and the gold slit eyes found me and dropped to what hung from my hand. Blood had dried along the jaw. The dead blue eyes caught the light.

He crossed the room slowly, his boots clicking on black stone, and stopped five feet from me, seven feet tall, looking down.

"Report."

I set the captain's head on the war table with a heavy thud. It rolled once and settled against a stack of maps, staring at the ceiling.

Then I told him everything: the Glade and the levels, integration reaching one hundred percent on the ridge, the shift-change gap Shiryeon had spotted, my decision to go in alone. The drop. The barracks. The forty mages and their beam. The captain's blessed steel, the nine veterans, the strike I hadn't let him finish. The bombardment of every military target, and the civilian quarter left standing.

"Thirteen minutes," I finished.

He hadn't moved once while I spoke. Not at the mages, not at the captain, not at the bombardment. When I stopped, the room held its silence for ten seconds.

Then he walked to the table, picked up the head, and turned it slowly in his claws, studying the cut: the perfectly clean, impossibly thin line where the katana had passed through.

"One stroke," he said quietly.

He set it back down and went to the western map. One clawed finger traced the supply road from Harren to the front.

"The western garrison holds approximately fourteen days of reserves. Without Harren, eighteen thousand imperial soldiers begin starving within two weeks. Their mages run out of reagents within ten days. Weapon maintenance ceases within seven." His finger tapped the front line once. "You have crippled an entire theater of war in thirteen minutes."

When he turned back, the gold eyes had narrowed slightly. It wasn't displeasure. It was something rarer, and it took me a moment to recognize it as satisfaction.

"You exceeded the parameters of the mission. You completed it in a fraction of the allotted time. You neutralized a blessed-steel wielder and forty combat mages without sustaining meaningful injury." A glance at the table. "And you brought me a trophy." A pause. "Theatrical. But effective for morale."

He leaned forward with both hands flat on the war table. The orbs threw sharp shadows across his face.

"Not five days ago you were level one," he said. "You could not kill a lesser minotaur cleanly. Tonight you dismantled a garrison single-handedly. You are already stronger than every field commander I possess, save Shiryeon." He let that sit in the air. In the doorway, Skeev had stopped breathing. "Your current level?"

He asked it plainly. A commander requesting a data point from an asset.

I called up the window, and it hung in the air where only I could see it.

【STATUS】
Name: Mephesto
Class: Demon Lord's Apostle
Level: 42
XP: 14,940 / 18,000
HP: 1,040 / 1,040 | MP: 1,080 / 1,080
Attack: 985 | Agility: 975 | Stamina: 1,025
Unique Skill: Dark Matter Manipulation
Passive: All experience earned is doubled.

I dismissed it.

"Forty-two."

The room went still. From the doorway came a strangled squeak. Skeev's mouth was opening and closing without producing a word.

The Demon Lord didn't move. For the first time since I'd known him, he was truly silent. It wasn't the calculated pause of a strategist choosing his next words. It was the silence of someone recalculating fundamental assumptions.

Five seconds. Ten.

He turned slowly to the western map. Then the eastern. Then the southern. His claws tapped once against the table.

"Forty-two," he repeated, almost to himself.

He walked to the window. Black earth stretched to the horizon under the red sky, and his reflection looked back at him from the dark glass.

"My strongest field commander is Shiryeon. Level eighty-nine." He spoke without turning. "My generals range from fifty to seventy. The heroes the goddess summoned will likely reach similar levels within months, given proper training and divine blessing." Now he turned. "You have reached forty-two in days. Without divine blessing. Without formal training. Through raw combat and self-engineered biology."

Something dangerous and delighted moved in his eyes.

"At this rate, Mephesto, you will surpass my generals within the week."

He sat down at the head of the war table and folded his hands.

"Rest tonight. Tomorrow we speak of what comes next. The western front is crippled, but there are other theaters that require attention." His eyes flicked to the head. "Leave that. I'll have it mounted."

"Yes, Demon Lord." I bowed and left. The black doors closed behind me with a resonant boom.

Skeev scurried after me, practically vibrating.

"Forty-two! Master was level one when Skeev first met Master! Skeev remembers! Master couldn't even... well, Master was still very impressive, very scary, Skeev always knew Master was..."

"Skeev."

He snapped his mouth shut.

The castle had sunk into the deep hours. Pale blue torches lined the corridors, and the servants we passed bowed low, and a few of them stole glances. Word of Harren had spread already, and I could see it in their faces. It was a new kind of deference. It wasn't fear of the Demon Lord's pet project anymore. It was something earned.

At my door, Skeev took up his position without being asked. He puffed out his small green chest and gripped his tiny spear with both hands.

"Skeev guards. Master rests. Nobody gets through. Nobody."

The first thing I did was get Harren off me.

Dark matter flowed from my pores in a thin film, millions of microscopic clusters sweeping across my skin like living liquid. They dissolved the dirt, the dried blood, the sweat, and pulled it all into condensed pellets that I flicked into a stone basin. I ran the film through my hair, across my face, and down the arm where the captain's blood had sprayed. Thirty seconds later I was cleaner than any bath could have made me. It needed no water at all.

Then, because I was me, I ran an experiment.

The theory of a shadow clone was simple: project a large volume of dark matter outward, shape it into a body, and sustain it with a continuous mana feed. In practice, the demands were enormous.

Black tendrils coiled out of my arms, chest, and back, thickened, and converged two feet in front of me into a rough shape: a torso, limbs, a head. I fed mana through my blood into the clusters, and it firmed up, shoulders broadening and legs taking form, until a featureless black silhouette of my height stood wavering in front of me like smoke in a draft.

Then the drain hit.

My MP plummeted. The clone was pulling mana at a vicious rate just to hold itself together. The legs lost definition, and the head collapsed into a blob. I gritted my teeth and pushed, and it snapped back into shape for two seconds, three. Then the left arm came apart entirely, and the torso caved in.

I let it go. The dark matter rushed back into me like a receding tide.

Eight seconds, and nearly a third of my mana pool gone. Keeping billions of clusters running in a separate body while my own kept running was beyond me at forty-two. But it had held its shape, briefly, and that was the point of the test. The concept was sound. It needed scale I didn't have yet. Perhaps around level sixty, or sooner if I redesigned the circulation for external constructs.

I filed it away and lay down on the cold stone bed.

Sleep took me like a stone sinking into black water. My body didn't rest the way a body should. The clusters shifted into full autonomous repair. They mended the hairline fractures in three knuckles from the speed strikes, the micro-tears in my shoulder tendons from the drop, and the faint scarring in the mana channels of my forearms from the needle barrage. Ambient demonic energy seeped through the walls and floor into me like water into a sponge. Around the fourth hour, a neural efficiency pass shaved a fraction off my reaction time. By the sixth, my reserves were full.

I woke a little stronger than I'd lain down.

The red sky had brightened to a harsh blood-orange when small knuckles tapped on the door.

"Master?" Skeev's voice was hoarse from a whole night on his feet. "Master, the sun is up. Skeev guarded all night. Nobody got through. Not one person. Well. Bris tried to bring breakfast, but Skeev told her to wait, and she is waiting outside with a tray, and it smells very good, and Skeev is very hungry, but Skeev did not eat any, because it is Master's food." A pause. "Is Master awake?"

I opened the door. Skeev stood rigid as a plank with his spear across his chest and his yellow eyes bloodshot. Behind him was Bris Hollowpot, lavender-skinned and flour-dusted, her floppy ears lifting when she saw me. She balanced a tray as large as she was.

"Master Mephesto! The whole kitchen heard about Harren! An entire depot! So I brought the good stuff. Extra portions." Steam rose from slabs of seared basilisk flank, a mound of roasted roots glazed in something dark and sweet, three hardboiled wyvern eggs, black-grain porridge, and a pitcher of something deep purple.

"Thank you, Skeev," I said. "Come and eat with me."

Skeev froze. His lower lip trembled.

"Master... wants Skeev to eat with Master?" He looked at Bris. Bris looked at him. Neither of them seemed to know what to do with this. "Skeev has never eaten at a master's table. Skeev eats scraps in the servant hall. With the other goblins. On the floor."

Bris recovered first. She hustled past me, set the tray down on the little table by the balcony, and laid everything out with practiced speed. "I'll get another plate!" Then she was gone down the corridor at a stubby-legged sprint.

Skeev climbed into the chair opposite me. He had to kneel on it to reach the table, and he sat with both hands flat on the stone, staring at the food as if it might vanish. When Bris came back and heaped his plate with basilisk and porridge, he stared at it, then at me.

"Skeev will remember this forever," he said quietly.

He picked up a piece of basilisk with both hands, took a bite, and closed his eyes.

The basilisk was rich and dense, and my clusters went to work on it before it reached my stomach. The wyvern eggs were chalky on the outside, but the yolks were molten and savory. The purple drink was fermented darkberry juice, tart and strong. Bris hovered at my elbow refilling plates and glowing.

"Is it good? I tried a new glaze. Shadow honey from the northern hives. Very expensive. Chef Mauk will yell at me later, but I don't care, because Master destroyed a whole human depot and deserves shadow honey."

"Bris," I said. "Sit down with us. It stays between the three of us."

The pitcher stopped halfway to my cup. Her dark eyes blinked rapidly. "Sit? At the table?"

She looked at Skeev. Skeev, cheeks stuffed with basilisk, nodded vigorously. "Master is very generous," he mumbled. "Best master."

Bris set the pitcher down slowly. She glanced toward the corridor, untied her apron, folded it over the back of a chair, and climbed up beside Skeev, her short legs dangling well above the floor. For a moment she simply sat with her hands in her lap, looking at the food she'd cooked as if she'd never seen it from this side.

"I've never sat at a table like this," she said softly. "I always stand. I serve. That's what imps do in the castle."

She took a small bite of basilisk with trembling fingers. Her round face scrunched up, and her eyes went wet.

"It's good," she whispered. "My food is actually good."

Skeev patted her on the back with a greasy hand. "Skeev always said so. Bris never believed."

We ate in the red morning light, and little by little she loosened up and started on the kitchen gossip. Chef Mauk was a tyrant who took credit for her recipes. The spice stores were running low because caravans from the northern territories were late. The generals never complimented the food.

"But Master's different," she said, pointing a root vegetable at me. "I knew from the first night. Master asked me questions. Nobody asks Bris questions. They say bring food, and Bris brings food."

Skeev nodded solemnly. "Same for Skeev. Before Master, Skeev was a gate goblin. Nobody remembered Skeev's name."

Something passed between the two of them, a servant and a cook. It was a quiet kind of solidarity, and I didn't interrupt it.

Then Bris stiffened. "I have to go. If Mauk finds the kitchen empty, he'll scream for an hour." The apron went back on in one motion, and she stacked the plates at speed. At the door she turned back. "I'll keep this between us. But I'm making Master something special tonight." A shy smile, and she was gone with the plates rattling.

Skeev turned his earnest yellow eyes on me. "Master sees the Demon Lord today for the next mission, yes? Skeev will escort Master whenever Master is ready."

"Not exactly. I'm going to make a request." I looked at his bloodshot eyes. "And I'm ordering you to go rest. Come back to my side later today."

His ears drooped. "But Skeev does not like leaving Master's side. What if someone bothers Master? What if..."

I gave him a look.

"Skeev is going. Skeev is resting. Right now." He hopped down, wobbled as the night caught up with him, and picked up his spear. At the threshold he turned. "Master will call for Skeev when Master needs Skeev?"

"I will."

He shuffled away toward the servants' quarters. Down the corridor I heard him yawn, a small, squeaky sound that bounced off the obsidian.

The throne room was vast: black pillars rising into shadow and crimson light falling through narrow windows. The Demon Lord sat on the throne this time with one leg crossed over the other and his chin resting on his fist.

"Mephesto. You have a request."

It wasn't a question. He already knew.

"Yes, Demon Lord. I'd like a few days to myself, to train and to conduct some new experiments." I held his gaze. "And I'd like to know more about the place you spoke of. The one that holds an ancient power."

Something flickered across the gold eyes. It was recollection.

"You remember," he said quietly.

He uncrossed his legs and sat up straight.

"The Abyssal Spire. It predates the Celestine Empire. It predates most recorded history on this continent." His voice carried between the pillars. "Its heart lies beneath the Korath Mountains, under a peak the old tribes called the Hollow Fang, beyond the Blackstone Pass. But the Spire is older than the mountains' names, and its doors are not all where one would expect. The key I gave you opens its outer seal."

He rose and descended the steps slowly, his shadow stretching long across the floor.

"The Spire is a trial ground. What lies inside changes depending on who enters. It reads the intruder, their level, their abilities, their weaknesses, and builds its challenges accordingly." He stopped at the foot of the stairs. "I told you it required level twenty to enter without being destroyed. You are forty-two. The Spire will adjust. The trials will be significantly more dangerous than anything you would have faced at twenty."

The gold eyes narrowed slightly.

"But so will the reward."

He folded his hands behind his back. "As for your request, you have earned it. Take your days. Train. Experiment. Enter the Spire if you choose. I will assign you nothing until you return. The western front will bleed slowly without Harren. There is no urgency."

He returned to the throne and settled.

"Is there anything else?"

"No, my liege. And thank you."

I bowed and left, and the doors closed behind me with a boom that rolled down the corridor.

I stood alone in the hall with the castle humming around me: distant marching, the screech of wyverns outside, the low thrum of demonic energy running through the obsidian walls. I slid my hand into my coat and touched the obsidian key. It was cold and smooth, and it vibrated faintly against my fingertips, as if something inside it was alive and waiting.

Days to myself. No mission, no handlers. Just time.

The Spire could wait a little longer. First I had ideas to play with.
