Interlude

The Empty Seat

620 words · about 3 min

‹ Chapter 3 Contents Chapter 4 ›

Far to the west, in Celestos, the white palace was never fully dark.

Golden light lived in its walls. It rose from the marble floors and pooled in the high domes, and it made everyone who stood in it look a little holier than they were. Twenty young people from Lecture Hall 3 stood in it now, in a line, in borrowed white robes, waiting their turn to be touched by a goddess.

Seraphiel Auren moved down the line like warm weather. She was tall, gowned in light, with eyes the soft blue of a summer sky and a voice that made every word sound like a kindness. When she laid her palm on Souta Kirishima's brow, a shield of gold bloomed around him so brightly that half the line flinched. Holy Knight. Souta went to one knee, overcome, and the court sighed in approval.

Keisuke Hidaka received a mage's sight that sent runes scrolling across the cracked lenses of his glasses. Gouki Tanabe laughed out loud at the strength that flooded into his fists. Tetsu Arakawa lifted a greatsword that had been forged, the priests said, for him alone, three hundred years before he was born. Tsubaki Minase vanished into her own shadow and reappeared behind the high priest, delighted with herself. Rhys Hollander, the exchange student from London, turned a pair of daggers over in his hands and muttered, "Brilliant. Absolutely mental. Brilliant."

Shiori Kanzaki stood eleventh in line and counted.

She had counted on the first night, in the dormitory of white stone, while the others cried or prayed or argued about whether this was a coma. She had counted again at breakfast, and again this morning. The number never changed.

Twenty.

There had been twenty-one people in Lecture Hall 3.

When the goddess reached her, Shiori felt the touch like sunlight on closed eyelids, and something unfolded behind her eyes: a cool, clear lattice of lines and angles and probabilities. The goddess murmured the name of her class. Shiori bowed and said nothing, and later, when the others compared blessings over dinner, she didn't say it aloud.

At the end of the ceremony, while the court applauded, she did something no one else had thought to do. She stepped out of line.

"Lady Seraphiel." Her voice was quiet, but it carried. "There were twenty-one of us."

The applause stuttered. Priests turned. Souta hissed her name.

The goddess looked down at her, and her serenity didn't waver at all. "Twenty answered the call, child. Sometimes the passage between worlds is not kind to everyone. Grieve if you must. Then grow strong. Your world's sorrow is not this world's burden."

It was a beautiful answer, which was what bothered Shiori about it.

"The light changed around him," she said. "I saw it. It went red. He fell, and we rose."

For one moment, so brief that afterward Shiori could never be certain she hadn't imagined it, the warm blue eyes went still. It was the stillness of someone hearing a piece move on a board she had believed was empty.

Then the goddess smiled and laid one warm hand on Shiori's head, and the warmth was like a door closing gently in her face.

"Rest, little scholar. You have had a long journey."

That night, while the others slept, Shiori sat by the window of the white dormitory and opened the notebook she'd been holding when the floor split, the only thing she'd brought from Earth. Below the professor's diagram of a spinning galaxy, under the words 95% unseen, she wrote a single line and underlined it twice.

The twenty-first. Red light. Where did he go?

Then she closed the book and watched the strange stars until dawn.
