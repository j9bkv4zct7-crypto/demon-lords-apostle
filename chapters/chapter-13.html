Chapter 13

The Spire Door

1,969 words · about 9 min

‹ Chapter 12 Contents End of Volume 1 ›

The ogres at the castle gate bowed their horned heads without a word as we passed. Since Harren, the story had gone ahead of me through the castle: thirteen minutes, a garrison reduced to nothing, and the Apostle who came back with a severed head and now walked the halls in a coat made of darkness.

At my door, Skeev took up his post.

"Skeev will wake you when the shift changes, my lord. Five hours, about." He hesitated. "Shall Skeev arrange a meeting with the Demon Lord for the morning? About the Spire?"

"Do that. And bring incense while I sleep. Keep it lit."

"Incense and an audience with the Demon Lord. Consider it done, my lord." He bowed low, and his small footsteps pattered away down the corridor until I couldn't hear them.

Inside, the thick stone walls swallowed every sound but the hum of twenty-eight loops cycling behind their limiters. I let the coat and pauldrons sink into my blood, stored in the anchors for instant recall, and left only a thin second skin of dark matter. I lay down on the furs. The weight of the day settled into my muscles: the second layer, the limiters, the three modes, the sealed pearl at my hip. My body had been pushed, rebuilt, and pushed again.

A soft knock came, and the door opened a crack. A small lavender hand set a clay dish on the floor just inside the threshold. I caught a glimpse of a round face and nervous dark eyes, and Bris withdrew without a word. The dish held three cones of dark amber incense.

They lit themselves. A thin curl of smoke rose and filled the room with the same deep, earthy fragrance Thalia had chosen. It gave warmth without heat and weight without pressure. My clusters noted the effect: a mild sedative that hastened cellular recovery and smoothed neural activity.

My breathing slowed. Behind the limiters, the loops went on compounding, banking power while I rested, and the second layer sank into permanence like roots growing into stone.

Sleep took me.

"My lord." Skeev's voice was gentle and clear through the iron. "The shift has changed. The Demon Lord will receive you in the throne room at your convenience."

The incense had burned down to pale ash. The narrow window showed the same blood-red sky, a little brighter now. It was what passed for morning here.

My body felt different. It wasn't dramatic. It was subtle. The anchors were fully seated, and the second layer wasn't new architecture anymore. It was simply part of me, as natural as breathing. The debuff was gone.

I sat up and thought, and the coat came over me in one smooth wave, crystal and pauldrons and the sealed pocket at my hip, in under a second.

One last look before I went.

The level hadn't moved. Everything underneath it had. The loops had spent the whole night compounding on the base, and my reserves had doubled while I slept.

The number at the top was the least true thing on the page.

I dismissed it.

Skeev waited in the corridor, freshly groomed, his tunic clean and his yellow eyes bright. "Shall we go to the throne room, my lord?"

"Let's go."

The doors opened inward before anyone touched them. It was the Demon Lord's will.

Purple flame burned along the walls of the throne room this morning, and black pillars rose into the dark. The air was heavier than anywhere else in the castle, thick with his ambient mana, a pressure on my skin like the bottom of a deep ocean.

He sat on his throne of black iron and bone with one leg crossed over the other and his chin on his fist. The golden hair and the yellow horns caught the purple light, and the gold slit eyes were already on me, as if he'd been watching since before the doors opened.

"Mephesto." His voice filled the room without effort. "You look different. Stronger." The corner of his mouth moved, very faintly. "Sit."

A chair of dark stone rose out of the floor before the throne. Behind me, Skeev stayed by the doors, pressed against the wall and trembling.

"Thank you, my liege."

The chair was cold, smooth, and precisely shaped, as if the castle had measured me before it made it. He watched me settle. His eyes traced the coat, the crystal, and the sealed pocket at my hip, reading me the way a master craftsman reads a blade he has forged.

"The recursion has seated," he said. "Twenty-eight nested loops. Sereth reported a zero percent rejection rate." He uncrossed his legs and leaned forward slightly. "In all my long reign, I have never seen a mortal body accept that architecture without losing something. A limb. A mind. A life." A pause. "You lost nothing."

The purple light moved across his face, and his expression gave nothing away.

"You wanted to see me. Speak."

Even sitting still and doing nothing, he put out more raw power than anything I had ever felt. Pressing against my limiters, it made my twenty-eight loops at full output seem like a candle held up to the sun.

"I've finished the training I needed," I said. "I ask your permission to take on the Spire."

The gold eyes narrowed. It wasn't displeasure. It was assessment.

"Already," he said quietly.

He rose without hurry and without a sound, came down the three steps of the dais, and walked toward me. His mana thickened around me like an incoming tide. He stopped six feet away and looked down.

"A week ago you were level one." His eyes held mine. "Now you sit before me in an architecture that has killed greater demons. You carry a Voidstone. You have twenty-eight recursive loops nested in a mortal frame that should have shattered at twelve."

He turned and walked slowly to the left, his golden hair shifting across his shoulders.

"The Abyssal Spire is not a training ground, Mephesto. It is a tomb. Every floor is a trial built to break whoever enters. It will read you and shape itself to you, but it will not lower itself for you. Floor one begins at level forty." He glanced back over his shoulder. "Floor ten houses something at level two hundred."

He let that settle.

"You have the key. Permission was granted when I gave it to you." He faced me fully. "But know this. The Spire does not care what you have survived. It tests what you are willing to become." He returned to the throne and sat. "When do you intend to enter?"

"Immediately," I said. "I've engineered unlimited mana pools and built enhancement algorithms that raise my power further. I'm on another level entirely, beyond what my level says."

He didn't move. His chin went back to his fist. In a being less controlled, the look in his eyes might have been called curiosity.

"Unlimited mana pools," he repeated, flat and measured. "Enhancement algorithms. Beyond what your level says."

Silence. The purple torches flickered.

Then the Demon Lord laughed. It wasn't loud or mocking. It was a single low breath through his nose, the closest thing to genuine amusement I had ever seen from him.

"You remind me of myself," he said. "Long ago. Before I learned the difference between engineering power and surviving what power attracts."

He stood again. This time he didn't come toward me. He went to the great arched window behind the throne, a jagged opening in the black stone over the endless red sky and the black lands below, and his silhouette filled it.

"Go, then. Immediately." His voice was final. "But hear me, Mephesto. The Spire does not test your mana pools. It does not care about your algorithms, your loops, or your limiters. It finds the fracture in your mind, the thing you refuse to look at, and it puts it in front of you wearing teeth."

He turned his head, and one gold eye caught the firelight.

"If you die in there, I will be disappointed. I have invested considerable resources in you." A pause. "Do not make me regret that investment."

One hand lifted in dismissal.

"The nearest of the Spire's doors lies beneath the eastern tower. This castle was raised over it. You have the key. Skeev will show you the way."

At the far end of the throne room, Skeev made a sound like a small animal being stepped on.

I rose. "I'll return once I've completed the Spire."

The chair sank back into the floor behind me as if it had never been there. He said nothing more. He only watched me walk the length of the hall with the purple light running along my coat, and the obsidian doors opened before me.

Skeev was pressed flat against the corridor wall, a shade paler than usual.

"The... the Spire, my lord?" His voice cracked. "Now? Right now? This very moment?"

I kept walking. He scrambled after me with his short legs pumping.

"The eastern tower. Yes. Skeev knows the way. Of course Skeev knows the way. Skeev has never been inside, naturally, because Skeev values his life, and Skeev is level three, and everything in there would eat Skeev before Skeev finished screaming. But Skeev knows where the door is, yes, absolutely..."

He talked the whole way. Down the central corridor, past the armory where demon knights sharpened black iron, through a narrow passage that dropped steeply while the walls grew rougher and older and were carved with symbols older than the castle itself. The air changed. It grew colder and denser. The mana down here didn't feel like the Demon Lord's. It wasn't oppressive. It was ancient, patient, and waiting.

The passage ended in a circular chamber. Its ceiling was lost in darkness. Its walls were obsidian polished to a mirror, and they threw my reflection back at me from every side: a hundred figures in black coats, with violet light under their skin.

In the center stood a door.

It was twelve feet tall and made of black stone, with no handle and no hinges. At its center was a single keyhole, a narrow vertical slit. Faint purple light pulsed from the seam, slow, like breathing. Like something alive.

Skeev stopped ten feet back. For the first time since we'd left the throne room, he had nothing to say.

"This is as far as Skeev goes, my lord." He swallowed hard. His hands were clasped together, and his yellow eyes glistened in the purple light. "Please come back."

He meant it. Every word.

I'd come to this world wanting one thing, the power to hold the universe's darkest matter in my hand. I hadn't counted on anyone waiting at a door for me. I looked at the small goblin who had guarded it every night since he'd first knocked on it, and found that I didn't mind it at all.

"I'll see you when I return," I said.

I turned to the door and took the obsidian key from my coat. It was cold and smooth, and it thrummed against my fingers as if it had been waiting a very long time for this exact moment. The slit drank the light as I raised it.

I slid the key in.

The purple seam flared once, the way an eye opens.

And I turned it.
