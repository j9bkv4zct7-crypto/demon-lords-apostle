Chapter 1

The Wrong Light

3,184 words · about 14 min

‹ Home Contents Chapter 2 ›

People like to say I was stolen.

The goddess says it. Her heroes say it. In the years since, I've heard it whispered in taverns and screamed across battlefields: the stolen hero, the one the Demon Lord took.

They have it backward. I wasn't stolen. My wish was granted.

I just didn't know I'd made one.

It was a Thursday, the last lecture of the afternoon, and Professor Hanazawa was talking about the dark.

"Ninety-five percent," he said, tapping the projector screen with a laser pointer that had been dying since April. The red dot flickered across a spiral galaxy. "Ninety-five percent of the universe is something we cannot see, cannot touch, and cannot explain. Dark energy. Dark matter. We know it's there because galaxies spin too fast. Without it, they would fly apart. With it..." He shrugged, and the shrug said thirty years of grant applications. "With it, the math holds. Mostly."

Lecture Hall 3 had seats for eighty. There were twenty-one of us, scattered across the tiers the way people scatter when they've had the same seats for two years and no longer pretend to like each other. The fluorescent lights hummed. Somebody's phone buzzed against a desk, and nobody looked up.

I was twenty-one. I sat in the second-to-last row, left side, by the window that didn't open. I had a part-time shift at a logistics warehouse starting at seven, a rent payment I could make if nothing broke, and a notebook full of equations I understood better than I understood most people.

I watched the professor more than the slides. He was maybe fifty, though he looked older. Grey at the temples, a coffee stain he'd been wearing for a week, the posture of a man who had wanted something enormous once and settled for tenure. Every day he went home, came back, and explained the invisible to bored kids.

That was the future the world had laid out for me, as neat as a lab report. Graduate. Get hired. Work until the work was all there was. Become a man who lectured about the most powerful force in the universe and couldn't move a single grain of it.

Galaxies would fly apart without it, I wrote in the margin. Then, because I was tired and nobody was watching: Imagine holding it.

Three rows down, Shiori Kanzaki raised her hand.

She always sat front row, center, with her black hair tied back and three colors of pen lined up like surgical instruments. Top of every exam since first year. She didn't talk much, but when she did, professors listened, and the rest of us felt slightly stupid.

"If dark matter only interacts through gravity," she said, "then in principle, anything that could manipulate gravity directly could manipulate dark matter."

Professor Hanazawa smiled for the first time all semester. "In principle, Kanzaki-san. In principle, a lot of things."

She turned her head slightly, as if she'd felt someone looking. For half a second her eyes met mine across three rows. Cool, assessing, the way she looked at a problem set she'd already solved.

Then she turned back, and the floor split open.

It began as a line. A thin seam of light, thinner than a hair, drawn across the linoleum from one wall to the other. I remember thinking, absurdly, that's a laser, because that was the only kind of light I'd ever seen behave that way.

Then it widened.

Gold poured up out of the floor like the building was sitting on top of the sun. Desks lifted. Paper whirled. Souta Kirishima, our class president (the department called it cohort representative; nobody else did), was on his feet shouting for everyone to stay calm, which is the kind of thing Souta would shout in an earthquake, a fire, or the end of the world. Gouki Tanabe, all six-foot-three of him, grabbed the edge of his desk as if he could hold the room down by force.

The light swallowed everything. Walls, voices, the dying laser pointer, Professor Hanazawa's mouth open in an O.

And then a voice, from everywhere and nowhere, warm as honey and impossibly far away:

"Chosen ones of another world, we summon you to aid us in our time of need..."

I was weightless.

I hung in a sea of gold, and all around me my classmates hung too, frozen in poses of fear and wonder. Souta with one hand out. Keisuke Hidaka clutching his glasses. Shiori, eyes wide open, completely calm, looking around as if she were already taking notes.

The light was warm. It felt like being chosen.

Then the warmth went out of it.

It happened at the edges first: a cooling, like a cloud crossing the sun. The gold around me deepened to amber, then to the dull red of an emergency light. A chill climbed my spine. Somewhere very far away, something that was not the goddess was paying attention.

And I began to fall.

The others rose. I watched them drift upward into the gold, growing smaller, their voices thinning into the glow. I fell the other way, into red, into dark.

The last thing I saw was Shiori Kanzaki, high above me, turning in the light. She was looking straight down at me. Her lips moved. I couldn't hear the word, but I saw her face change as she watched the light around me turn the color of blood.

She saw it. Out of twenty people, she was the one who saw.

Then the red closed over me, and there was nothing at all.

I woke on cold marble.

Black marble, polished to a mirror, so cold it ached through my shirt. The ceiling above me was lost in shadow. The air was thick and heavy, and it tasted faintly of iron. I lay there and catalogued, because cataloguing is what I do when I'm afraid. Temperature: low. Pressure: wrong somehow, as if the atmosphere had more weight to it. Light source: none.

Then torches flared to life, one by one, in a long double row, each burning with a cold blue flame.

They revealed a throne room built for giants. Black columns rose into the dark. A red velvet carpet ran from where I lay to a dais at the far end, and on the dais sat a throne carved from a single block of obsidian.

Someone was sitting on it.

He rose as I watched. He was tall, at least seven feet, and when he stepped into the torchlight I saw golden hair that fell past his shoulders, and two curved horns, yellow as old ivory, sweeping back from his temples. He wore a long black coat that moved like smoke. His face was beautiful and uncomfortably human, apart from the eyes.

The eyes were gold, slit like a cat's, and they burned like embers in a forge.

"So," he said. His voice was deep and smooth, and it filled the hall without any effort. "The goddess's summoning was a success. How fortunate." He descended the steps, and every footfall echoed. "She summoned her heroes..."

He stopped above me and smiled faintly.

"...and I stole one."

I pushed myself up to sitting. My classmates were gone. The light was gone. The voice that had called us chosen was gone.

"Welcome, human," he said. "You were meant to fight me. Instead, you will fight for me." He inclined his head a precise degree, the courtesy of a man who could afford to be courteous. "I am the Demon Lord, Zarvoth Kael-Morr."

I should have been terrified. Some part of me was. My heart was going like a hammer, and my hands were cold. But underneath the fear something else was rising, quiet and bright. It felt like the moment a proof comes together and you can see the whole shape of it at once.

The lecture hall was gone, and so was the warehouse shift at seven. Professor Hanazawa's tired future was gone with them.

"Ah, yes." The Demon Lord waved one clawed hand as if remembering a minor formality. "Say Status Window and you'll see your stats. And I will grant you one power. Name anything. But only one."

I got to my feet. I'm not short, but my eyes were level with his chest. I looked up.

"I don't mean to be rude, my new liege," I said. "But any power I desire?"

He tilted his head. Something like amusement moved behind those gold eyes.

"Any power," he said. "Within reason, of course. I am generous, not foolish."

He turned and walked back toward the throne, coat trailing, torchlight sliding along his horns.

"Let me be clear, human. The goddess Seraphiel Auren has summoned your former classmates. Twenty of them. Each has been gifted a unique class and abilities by her hand. They will grow stronger. They will come for me." He sat, crossed one leg over the other, and rested his chin on his fist. "And when they do, I need you to be ready."

Somewhere deep in the castle, something growled. It was low and distant, and it sounded hungry.

"So yes. Ask for anything. A weapon that never breaks. Dominion over shadows. The power to devour the skills of those you defeat. Command over the dead. Whatever your mind can conjure." His eyes narrowed a fraction. "But choose wisely. You get only one, and it will define what you become in this world."

The torches flickered. He waited with the patience of something that had been alive for centuries and expected to be alive for centuries more.

"So then, my new Apostle," he said quietly. "What power do you desire?"

Ninety-five percent of the universe is something we cannot see, cannot touch, and cannot explain. It holds galaxies together, and it bends light. If there was a single force underneath everything else, this was the one.

Imagine holding it.

"I want the power of Dark Matter Manipulation," I said, "my new liege."

For the first time, his composure moved. The gold eyes widened, just slightly. Then a smile spread across his face. It wasn't warm, but it was real, and it looked deeply impressed.

"Dark Matter Manipulation," he murmured, letting the words hang in the air. He rose and came down the steps again. "You don't think small, do you?"

He stopped in front of me and lifted one hand. Black and violet energy coiled around his claws, crackling, and the air itself began to hum, a low vibration I felt in my teeth.

"Very well. I will grant it. But understand this. At your current level, you are an infant holding a blade. Dark matter is among the most volatile and destructive forces in existence. You will be able to shape it, condense it, and weaponize it, but only as far as your body and your level can bear. Push beyond your limits and it will consume you from the inside out."

He pressed two fingers to my forehead.

The world went black.

Then the pain came. It felt like my bones were being hollowed out and refilled with something cold and dense and impossibly heavy, like neutron-star matter poured into a human mold. My veins burned purple beneath my skin. I couldn't scream. I didn't have the air.

Then it stopped.

I was on one knee on the black marble, gasping. My hands shook. And I could feel it: something coiled deep in my core, just beneath the sternum. It was heavy and hungry, and it was waiting.

"It is done," the Demon Lord said, looking down at me with calm satisfaction. "At level one, you can form small constructs. Projectiles, shields, perhaps a blade. As you grow, so will your control. At higher levels..." He paused. "Well. Let us simply say that dark matter can bend space itself."

He returned to his throne.

"Now. Say it, and see what you have become, Apostle."

The roar came again from deep in the castle, closer this time.

I stood. My legs held. I placed one hand over my heart and bowed, low and deliberate, and when I spoke my voice came out colder than I'd ever heard it.

"I will not fail you, my liege." Then: "Status Window."

A translucent pane of deep violet light unfolded in the air before my eyes. I knew instinctively that only I could see it. Glowing script etched itself across it line by line.

【STATUS】
Name: Mephesto
Class: Demon Lord's Apostle
Level: 1 | XP: 0 / 100
HP: 100 / 100 | MP: 50 / 50
Attack: 15 | Agility: 12 | Stamina: 10
Unique Skill: Dark Matter Manipulation
Create, shape, and weaponize dark matter. Current level permits small constructs: projectiles, thin shields, short blades. Higher levels unlock density control, gravitational distortion, and spatial compression.
Passive: All experience earned is doubled.

I read it twice. Then I dismissed it with a thought, and it dissolved like smoke.

Level one, with fifteen attack. A middling high-school athlete could probably beat me in a fistfight. But the passive at the bottom was the line that mattered. Doubled. Everything I earned in this world, I would earn twice as fast as anyone else.

Exponential growth is the most dangerous thing in the universe. I'd known that since I was twelve.

The Demon Lord watched me from his throne, chin on fist, gold eyes gleaming with quiet approval. I was still bowing.

"Good," he said simply. "Loyalty is rare. Even among demons."

He snapped his fingers. A side door groaned open, and a woman stepped through.

She was tall, close to six feet, lean as a drawn blade. Her skin was ashen grey. Her white hair was cropped short and shaved on one side, baring a pointed ear. She wore close-fitting black leather etched with crimson runes, and two curved daggers rode her hips. A long scar ran diagonally across the bridge of her nose and down her left cheek, pale against the grey.

And her eyes were amber: sharp, hot, and wholly unimpressed.

"This is Shiryeon," the Demon Lord said. "My commander. She will show you to your quarters and brief you on the state of the war."

Shiryeon looked me up and down the way a butcher looks at a cut of meat he's decided not to buy.

"So this is the stolen hero," she said. Her voice was flat and sharp, a blade drawn across stone. "Doesn't look like much."

She turned on her heel without waiting for an answer.

"Follow me, human. Try to keep up."

The last thing I saw before the doors closed was the Demon Lord's faint smile.

I followed her into a corridor of black stone lit by blue-flame torches. Through the narrow windows I caught glimpses of the outside world: blackened earth, jagged rock, and above it all a sky the color of a fresh wound. It wasn't sunset. That was simply the color of the sky here.

A simple human, she'd said. I reached for the cold weight beneath my sternum.

It came reluctantly, like dragging something heavy through tar. Then a blade formed in my palm, a dagger's length of pure black with faint violet edges. The air around it shimmered and bent, the way air bends over hot asphalt, except this was cold. I balanced it on one fingertip and spun it.

"A simple human?" I said to her back. "We'll see how quickly your tune changes."

Shiryeon stopped mid-stride. Her pointed ears twitched. She glanced over her shoulder, and her amber eyes found the spinning blade.

For a heartbeat something crossed her face. It wasn't fear. It was recognition.

"Dark matter," she said quietly, almost to herself.

Then the mask came back, and she walked on.

"Parlor tricks won't impress me, human. I've served His Majesty for two hundred years. I've watched warriors far stronger than you rot in unmarked graves." Her boots rang on the stone. "You're level one. Right now, a goblin pack could tear you apart. So before you start making threats with your little knife, understand something."

She turned so suddenly I almost walked into her. One hand rested on a dagger hilt. Her scarred face was inches from mine, and those amber eyes bored into me like hot iron.

"Out here, respect is earned in blood. Not words."

She held my gaze for a long moment. Then she shoved open a heavy iron door.

"Your quarters. Rest. Tomorrow, we begin."

I looked at her. At the scar, the cropped white hair, and the way she stood as if the floor belonged to her. In the lecture hall I would never have said what I said next. In the lecture hall I'd have looked away first.

But I was a long way from the lecture hall.

"When I'm strong enough," I said, "I'll marry you. When I become strong, you won't be able to refuse."

The corridor went silent.

"Don't worry," I added, because she seemed to need reassurance. "I have no ulterior motives. I wish to serve the Demon Lord and live a life with a beautiful demon such as yourself. You'll be my first wife." I considered. "I'd like to have at least two, though."

Shiryeon stared at me. For three full seconds, nothing in the castle moved.

Then she laughed.

It was a harsh, cracking sound, like ice breaking on a river, and it rang off the stone walls. She laughed so hard she had to brace one hand against the doorframe.

"You..." she gasped. "You've been here less than an hour. You're level one. You can barely form a dagger. And you're talking about marriage? Multiple wives?"

She wiped a tear from the corner of one amber eye with a clawed finger.

Then, like a switch thrown, the laughter died. Her face went flat and cold. I didn't see her draw. One moment the dagger was on her hip; the next its edge rested against my throat, runes glowing a faint red along the blade.

"Listen carefully, human," she said, low and dangerous. "I am not a prize to be won. I am not a goal on your little quest. I have gutted men twice your size for saying less."

She held it there for one long breath. Then she sheathed it in a single fluid motion.

"Become strong enough to survive tomorrow first. Then we'll see if you live long enough to embarrass yourself again."

She walked away down the corridor. Without looking back, she called out one last remark, and I could have sworn there was still a laugh buried somewhere in it.

"Two wives. Unbelievable."

I stood in the doorway of my new room and listened to her footsteps fade. A bed with dark sheets, a wooden desk, a basin of water, and a narrow window full of red sky.

That morning I had been a physics student with a warehouse shift and a tired future.

Tonight I was the Demon Lord's Apostle, and the most powerful force in the universe was sitting under my ribs, waiting to be learned.

I smiled and closed the door.
